Trading Card Guide

Pokemon cards are packed with clues. Learn how to read them, what the symbols mean, and how to take care of your collection.

  1. Name. What the Pokemon is called. Cards with the same name follow the "four copies" deck rule.
  2. HP (Hit Points). How much damage it can take before it is Knocked Out.
  3. Type symbol. The Pokemon's type, shown by an Energy symbol. Here, yellow means Lightning.
  4. Stage. Basic, Stage 1, or Stage 2. Stage 1 and 2 cards also say which Pokemon they evolve from.
  5. Energy cost. The little circles beside an attack show which Energy you must have attached to use it. A grey circle means any type of Energy will do.
  6. Weakness. A type this Pokemon takes extra damage from. Usually the damage is doubled.
  7. Resistance. A type this Pokemon takes less damage from. Usually 30 less.
  8. Retreat cost. How many Energy you discard to switch this Pokemon with one from your Bench. No circles means it retreats for free.
  9. Illustrator. The artist who drew the picture.
  10. Set number and rarity. The number shows which card it is within its set. The little shape shows how rare the card is.

Attack name and damage: the number on the right of an attack is how much damage it does. The words underneath may add a special effect.

Kinds of cards

Pokemon cards

The creatures that battle for you. Each has HP, attacks, and a type.

Energy cards

You attach Energy to your Pokemon to pay for attacks. Basic Energy matches a type. Special Energy gives extra effects.

Trainer cards

Helpers that you play from your hand. There are four kinds.

Trainer cards, up close

  • Item. Play as many as you like each turn. Examples include cards that find Pokemon or heal damage.
  • Supporter. Powerful helpers, but you can play only one Supporter each turn.
  • Stadium. Stays in play and affects both players. You can play one Stadium each turn, and a new one replaces the old one.
  • Pokemon Tool. Attach it to a Pokemon to give it a boost. A Pokemon can usually hold one Tool.

Basic, Stage 1, and Stage 2

Pokemon grow up, just like in the games.

Basic

Can be played straight from your hand onto the table. Every deck needs these.

Stage 1

Put it on top of the matching Basic Pokemon to evolve it.

Stage 2

Put it on top of the matching Stage 1 Pokemon. These are usually the strongest.

Some Pokemon skip a step. Some Basic Pokemon never evolve at all.

Special kinds of Pokemon cards

Cards with special names are usually powerful, but they come with a trade. If they are Knocked Out, your opponent takes extra Prize cards. Always read the card text to be sure.

Pokemon ex
Strong Pokemon from the Scarlet and Violet era. Your opponent takes 2 Prize cards if one is Knocked Out.
Tera Pokemon ex
A Pokemon ex with a Tera type. While on the Bench, it is protected from attack damage.
Pokemon V
Sword and Shield era Pokemon with big HP. Gives up 2 Prize cards.
Pokemon VMAX
A giant evolution of a Pokemon V. It gives up 3 Prize cards.
Pokemon VSTAR
An evolution of a Pokemon V with a special VSTAR Power you can use once per game. Gives up 2 Prize cards.
Pokemon GX and EX (older)
Older powerful cards from earlier eras. Many give up 2 Prize cards, but check the card.
Radiant Pokemon
Shiny-looking Basic Pokemon with strong abilities. You can have only one Radiant Pokemon in a deck.
Ancient and Future Pokemon
Pokemon from the Paradox Rift era. Some cards in the game only help one of these groups.
Full Art
Cards where the picture stretches over the whole card. These are mostly about looks.

What do the rarity symbols mean?

Down in the corner by the set number is a little shape. It tells you how hard the card is to find in a pack. Symbols can look a little different from set to set, so treat this as a friendly guide.

SymbolRarityWhat it means
CommonA black circle. The easiest cards to find in packs.
UncommonA black diamond. A little harder to find.
RareA black star. Hard to find. Often the stronger cards.
HoloHolo RareA star symbol with a shiny, rainbow picture that sparkles when you tilt the card.
Double Rare and Ultra RareTwo stars. Usually special powerful cards such as ex, often with fancy shiny art.
Illustration RareA gold star. The art spreads across the card, showing the Pokemon in its world.
Special Illustration RareTwo gold stars. Storybook-style art. Very hard to find.
Hyper RareThree gold stars. Often golden or sparkly cards. Among the rarest pulls.
PromoSpecial cards given out at events or in products. They may show a "PROMO" mark instead of a rarity shape.

A rare card is not always the best card to play with. Common cards can be great in a deck, and a rare card is not automatically valuable.

Card care and collecting tips

  • Use sleeves. Soft plastic sleeves protect cards from scratches and bent corners. Slide your favorites in right away.
  • Store in a binder. Binder pages with pockets keep cards flat and easy to show off. Do not squeeze too many in.
  • Do not bend or fold. Never bend cards, write on them, or stick tape on them.
  • Keep them dry and cool. Away from sun, water, snacks, and sticky fingers.
  • Trade fairly. Trade with friends only when both people are happy and a grown-up knows about it.
  • Collect what you love. The best collection is the one filled with Pokemon you like.
